At Bayer, every new business question tends to become a new dashboard. The result is hundreds of reports, conflicting KPI definitions, reports nobody owns, and a growing maintenance bill.

Reuse Gate sits before the build. It checks the existing reports first, and recommends reuse, repair, extension, migration, or a new build, with every decision backed by cited data. A person always approves.

Here, a business user asks for a Nubeqa sales dashboard in plain words. Reuse Gate asks what is unclear, then answers on one card: don't build. Extend an existing report that already covers two of the three KPIs.

The analyst sees every row behind that answer, and can turn it into a dashboard preview filled with real rows from the data.

For a new oncology build, the gates block release because the data model's owner is inactive. The approver simulates one active owner, all seven gates pass, and the decision is recorded with an owner and a review date.

A developer proposes a rule change, and it is measured on all one hundred and fifty requests before anyone may approve it.

Every role has its own view and sees only its own data, and approvers are notified by email.

On Bayer's synthetic data pack, fifty-seven of one hundred and fifty requests avoid a new build. That is thirty-eight percent fewer dashboards to build and maintain. One owner fix would unblock eighty more specs. These figures are modelled from the data, not measured savings.

It runs on Red Hat OpenShift AI, and the decisions are rules, not guesses, so they are repeatable and auditable. Build less. Build better.
